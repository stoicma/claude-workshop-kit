# MECE and the Rule of 3

## MECE

**M**utually **E**xclusive, **C**ollectively **E**xhaustive. Used to structure any list of supporting arguments, options, segments, or causes.

- **Mutually exclusive.** Items do not overlap. Each fact belongs to exactly one bucket.
- **Collectively exhaustive.** The buckets cover the whole space. Nothing important falls outside.

If buckets overlap, an executive will challenge the framing. If buckets miss something, an executive will spot the gap. Both undermine credibility.

## Common MECE structures

Pick the one that fits the question:

- **Time-based.** Past / present / future. Or short-term / medium-term / long-term.
- **Geographic.** EU / North America / APAC. Or country-by-country.
- **Functional.** Sales / operations / finance. Or front-office / middle / back.
- **Customer-based.** Segment A / segment B / segment C.
- **Product-based.** Line 1 / line 2 / line 3.
- **Value chain.** Sourcing / production / distribution / sales.
- **Cost vs revenue.** Cost reduction / revenue growth / capital efficiency.

If you cannot make the structure MECE, the structure is wrong. Pick a different decomposition.

## The Rule of 3

Three supporting points per argument. Almost always.

Reasons:
- Working memory holds 3-4 items reliably. Five and the audience starts to forget.
- Three feels complete. Two feels thin. Four needs justification.
- Three matches the visual rhythm of a slide grid (3 cards / 3 columns / 3 buckets).

When to break the rule:
- **Two** is acceptable when the binary genuinely is binary (build vs buy, Option A vs Option B). Do not force a third.
- **Four** is acceptable for a complete framework that the client already knows (the 4 P's, the 4 quadrants of a 2×2). Do not invent a fourth.
- **Five or more** means the slide has multiple messages. Split it.

## Combining MECE and Rule of 3

The deck's middle pyramid layer (the three pillars of the Resolution) should be both MECE and follow Rule of 3. So three pillars, no overlap, covering the whole answer.

Example. Resolution to "How do we enter the Western Balkans EV market?":

- **Pillar 1: Where to enter.** (geographic prioritization)
- **Pillar 2: How to enter.** (market entry mode: organic / partnership / acquisition)
- **Pillar 3: When to enter.** (sequencing and milestones)

Three pillars. They do not overlap. They cover the entire question. Each pillar then breaks into 3 supporting slides. Total: 9-12 evidence slides for the Resolution section, plus the divider.
