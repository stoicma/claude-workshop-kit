# Structure rules

## The Pyramid Principle

Lead with the answer. Support with three reasons. Back each reason with evidence.

The audience is a senior decision maker on a 30-minute calendar. They want your destination, not your journey. If they only read the title slides in order, they should still understand your recommendation and the logic behind it.

Pyramid layers:

1. **Top layer.** One sentence answering the question the executive asked. This is the deck's title and the slide 1 takeaway combined.
2. **Middle layer.** Three (occasionally two, rarely four) supporting arguments. Each becomes a section of the deck.
3. **Bottom layer.** Data, analysis, examples. One slide per supporting point usually, or a tight cluster.

## SCR storyline (Situation, Complication, Resolution)

Every deck has these three beats in order. The lengths vary but the order does not.

- **Situation (1-3 slides).** The shared starting point. What both you and the client agree is true today. No surprises here. This is the warm-up that gets everyone on the same page.
- **Complication (2-4 slides).** What changed, or what is at risk, or what the analysis surfaced that the client did not see. This creates the urgency. Quantify it. Numbers beat adjectives.
- **Resolution (10-20 slides).** Your recommendation, evidence, and implementation. Open this section with a divider slide that explicitly says "Our recommendation:" or "We recommend:". The resolution itself follows the Pyramid Principle: one-line conclusion, then three pillars, then evidence.

After the resolution, two optional sections:
- **Recommendations (2-4 slides).** Concrete next steps with ownership and timelines.
- **Appendix (open-ended).** Detailed analysis, methodology, raw data. Not for the reading-flow, only for the question-answer.

## Slide-count targets by deck type

| Deck type | Total slides | S | C | R | Recs | Appendix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Executive briefing | 8-12 | 1 | 2 | 5-8 | 1-2 | optional |
| Strategic recommendation | 15-25 | 2-3 | 3-4 | 8-15 | 2-3 | optional |
| Market entry / opportunity | 20-30 | 2 | 3 | 10-20 | 2-3 | yes |
| Transformation roadmap | 25-40 | 2-3 | 3 | 15-25 | 3-4 | yes |
| Status / steering report | 6-10 | 1 | 1-2 | 3-5 | 1 | rare |

## The title-only test

Before the deck ships, read only the action titles in order. Out loud. If they tell a complete coherent story without seeing any slide bodies, the structure is correct. If they sound like a list of topics ("Market overview", "Competitive landscape", "Our recommendation"), rewrite them as conclusions until the title sequence reads like an argument.

Reference: Slideuplift Exercise 5 (Title-Only Review). This is the load-bearing structural test.
